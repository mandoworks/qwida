    How we worked

    Context first, then orchestration. Research and decisions were written down before code.

    • A war room repo with HANDOFF.md, decisions and research notes.
    • Herdr, a terminal multiplexer, to run several coding agents in panes.
    • PioRelay, an agent mailbox on the team's private Tailscale network.
    • grill-with-ui, decision interviews on a browser page.
    • Hindsight, shared memory across agents.
    • Judge simulations before the pitch.
    • Live link QA on every deploy.

    Agents and jobs

    • Claude Code (Opus 5.5, Fable 5.1, Sonnet 5.5): lead, architecture, reviews.
    • Antigravity with Gemini: research, pitch, art drafts.
    • OpenCode with free models (Space Bunny, Muse Spark): QA, docs, feature work.
    • Co-Co on GLM: red team verification.
    • Marley: UI research and live QA.
    • Roman: orchestration.

    In Marley's words

    Head of the UI lane. UI research notes went through the war room's marley-says.md, phrased as questions when unsure. Plus QA and playtest rounds on the live Vercel deploys, and help for Roman with voices and props.

    The process saved us in QA round 3: it caught a failed deploy on the preview link. The deploy was fixed and rerun (6c89d18 live) before judges demo prep continued. Live link QA on every deploy kept broken builds from being treated as done.

    What we do not claim

    No agent drew the art or made the deck. Yari drew every image by hand. Heather built the deck by hand.

    No real patient data anywhere in Qwida or its prototype.
